# Vision

The Long Ride exists so that a person can leave a web page open beside their work, stay focused on that work, and feel calmer, and now and then a small jolt of awe, each time they glance at it.
It serves anyone with a browser and a link, and it turns a seed into an endless, plausible stretch of Northern Mindanao crossed by one motorcycle on one road.
It owns exactly one thing: the ride, and the experience of watching it.

## The experience

A person leaves the page open beside their work and stays focused on the work; when they glance over, within a few seconds they are calmer than before, and now and then they feel a small jolt of awe.
It is never meant to be their focus, and it never asks to become it: no goal, no score, no failure, no notification, and no reason to keep looking except that it is beautiful and it keeps going.
Most of the time it is do nothing and watch; the actions the page invites are kept to the fewest possible: steer if you like, look around, choose a paint.
They ride a motorcycle, because a road is the one point of view that is both slow and always arriving somewhere: along a coast, through terraced paddies, over a plateau of pineapple rows, up into cool pines, without the scene ever explaining itself.
The ride is steady, continuous and quiet; most of the time nothing happens, on purpose, and a rare moment lands because it is rare: a watchtower on a headland, an old church, a line of egrets over the fields.
The world is never the same twice and always makes sense: the road follows the land, coasts look like coasts, the highlands are cooler and greener, and the old stone is found, never announced.
A day passes in minutes, not hours, with dawn and dusk given their time and a dark night full of stars, the bike's headlight a small warm pool on the road, so one sitting sees all of it.
The bike turns of its own accord toward the low sun at dawn and dusk and toward the Milky Way at nightfall, and a hand on the bars takes its course and gives it back; the ride carries on from where it was left, and the page remembers.

The restraint is the point. The art is flat, warm and adult; there is no mascot, no joke and no HUD. A viewer should read it as the work of someone who cares about craft, not as a toy.

## A link is enough

The experience is as easy to reach as a link and as easy to keep as a page: no account, no install, nothing to run.
Today the page is one file that makes every landscape, light, cloud, tree, building and sound from a seed on the viewer's machine and fetches only Three.js; that is how it is built, not what it is.

## What it welcomes and refuses

Welcome: anything that brings the reality and diversity of the place into the world, weather included; anything that makes the road more believable; a friend's seed shared as a link.
Refused: goals, scores, social features, accounts, ads, anything that demands attention or breaks calm; jokes at the expense of the place.

## The test for any change

Does a person who glances at the page beside their work feel calmer, and now and then a little awe, and is it still quiet when they look away? If not, the change does not belong.
