# The Long Ride

The engine's rules, for agents and anyone changing `src/`. Most of them were learned by breaking something. The other docs each own one thing: `VISION.md` is what the page is for, the experience it is meant to create, and the test for any change; `CONTRIBUTING.md` holds every command (serve, bundle, checks, bench, capture, publish) and the scenery contract's prose; `docs/perf-notes.md` holds what a frame costs and what is left to win; `README.md` is the front door, with how to use the page and pointers. Do not repeat one file in another.

## Layout

A Three.js page with no assets and no dependencies. `index.html` is the page and the import map; `src/main.js` is the engine, `src/color-grade.js` the approved color grade, `src/noise.js` the CPU noise, and `src/water.js`, `src/milky-way.js` and `src/galaxy-matter.js` the approved water and night sky, `src/world.js` the terrain, climate and the road, `src/bike.js` the bike kit and `src/birds.js` the egret flock's kit; `library/` is the scenery (`contract.js`, `index.js`, one file per biome, species, site, prop, bird and paint). The browser loads them as modules from a static server. `tools/bundle.mjs` folds it all into `dist/index.html`, which `.github/workflows/pages.yml` publishes; `dist/` is generated and ignored, never edited. The bundler folds only static `import ... from` and `export const|let|function|class|default`, so keep modules to those forms, with no dynamic import or `import.meta` and top-level await only in the entry, and run the checks on the source page and on the bundle after an engine change. Keep every path relative so the page works under a project subpath. The Long Ride is an adaptation of Kun Chen's fly-with-me engine; his copyright line in `LICENSE` is never removed or altered. Three.js is pinned to r185.1 in the page's head; an upgrade is deliberate work with a visual diff, because the WebGPU renderer and TSL still rename things between monthly releases. `tools/` and `tests/` never ship. `assets/` holds the README's hero image only, and neither the bundler nor Pages touches it. `media/`, `review.html`, `review-assets/` and `.lavish/` are local review surfaces, gitignored.

## Terrain, climate and the road

- The CPU world (`src/world.js`) is the only terrain truth; the GPU only reads it through the heightfield window. Anything that needs a height (bike, camera, props, sound) reads `heightAt`, interpolating the exact rendered triangle, not bilinearly across a quad, or props float on steep terrain. The window holds only nine kilometres around the bike: for anything farther, call `world.sample`, never `biomeAt` or `fieldAt`.
- The road is `world.road`: rows of centre-line x and surface height every `CELL` metres along z, smoothed over a few hundred metres and never below `ROAD.floor`, carved into the land as a flat bench blending out to `ROAD.bank`. The GPU paints the asphalt, shoulders and lines from the same rows (`roadTex`), interpolated exactly as `road.x` does. Place nothing on the asphalt, shoulder or `VERGE`; bare-rock slope colour is kept off the bench.
- Biome color belongs to the actual triangle, not an interpolated corner: the main grid reconstructs its triangle samples in the fragment shader. When `colorNode` reads a color attribute, leave `vertexColors` off.
- One climate rule: `biomeWeights` on the CPU and the terrain `colorNode` on the GPU are the same soft cells in stretched climate space, measured from the nearest cell before the exponential so single precision never underflows. Biome centres must sit where the road's temperature, moisture and region actually fall (measure with `world.sample` along the road), or a landscape never appears. Placement reads the tree cell's centre, so a species must have weight in its cell's mix.
- Never write TSL arithmetic as a JS expression on a node (`number - node`, `0.07 + node`): it silently becomes `NaN` or a string and fails only in the shader compiler. Use `.add`, `.sub`, `.mul`.
- Placement RNG stays independent from asset-detail RNG. Grass bending reads immutable geometry-attribute height, never an already transformed `positionLocal.y`.

## Sky, light and time

- The day clock (`dayPhase`, `DAY_SECONDS`) and the sun's clock (`solar`) differ: `solar` runs at one pace by day and faster through a night that is `NIGHT_SHARE` of the cycle, blended over the twilights. Palette keys, `skyBodies`, and the sky events' spans and weights are all in solar phase; anything keyed to the sun's height reads `solar(dayPhase)` or the bodies, never the day clock. `INTRO.stretch` is a day-clock rate on top of that pace.
- Sky and fog share `horizonTint`, including its directional warmth and cloud crossing color; day palettes own both, and changing only a fog uniform breaks the boundary. Gentle local air gives way to complete fog before the streamed terrain ends; height fog and soft cloud volumes cover cloud crossings. Above the deck (`uAbove`) the tint is a haze that keeps the day's hue and its sunward warmth, never a flat cloud white, or the sun vanishes into it; only the whiteout goes white. The cloud sea is lit from a normal taken by finite differences of its own heap, on a ramp that follows the sun's height so it stays sculpted at noon, and the far sea fades into `horizonTint` like the sky.
- `uSunDir` is always the true sun, even below the horizon. One directional light is the sun by day and the moon by night; it may change direction only while its intensity is zero, and it starts only once the disc reaches the horizon, or trees throw shadows uphill from a sun below it.
- The approved Milky Way is `src/milky-way.js` plus `src/galaxy-matter.js`: fixed star instancing and generated stellar light, with one branching extinction field shared by both bakes and no catalog growth during the ride. The ride's night bearing comes from that same field, never a second copy. Keep `paintedClouds` shared by dome and stars; neither may draw over opaque clouds, and daylight and opaque deck crossings skip the star draw. No look switch ships. `tests/ride-checks.js` does not guard the sky: after sky changes run `node tests/galaxy-checks.mjs` and compare the night by eye at the same seed and vantage.

## Water

- `src/water.js` owns the approved jade water: its depth reads the exact CPU terrain triangle, its two ripple scales filter by pixel footprint, its light reads the shared day palette, and all motion follows simulation time. Keep it one opaque draw on the existing grid, with no reflection or depth passes and no new textures. Its record is `tests/water-review.md`; when isolating scenery for a capture, assign boolean visibility (`isLight === true`), since Three.js treats `visible = undefined` as visible.

## Scenery

- The library is the contributor surface and the engine is closed to it: entries are data, or `bake`/`build`/`place` functions over kits (tree, masonry, prop) that only produce vertex-colored geometry and positions. `validateLibrary` runs at load and `validateBaked` measures every baked geometry (the bike too, against `BIKE.triangles`); both must keep refusing colors outside the swatch envelope (judged in sRGB; a neutral near-white is a tint carrier), unknown ids and budgets, by entry name. A new capability for contributors is a new kit method or a new entry field with a validator rule, never a hole in the engine. `CONTRIBUTING.md` is the contract's prose; keep it and `library/contract.js` saying the same thing.
- Every species is one baked tree in three pools (wood, crown, distant crown) sharing the crown morph; the distant crown keeps every `keepEvery`-th card enlarged by `distantScale`. Detail levels never dither: the full crown morphs into the distant one over `CROWN_FADE`, and the crown assignment margins in `placeTrees` must exceed one ring rebuild step plus the camera's maximum orbit distance, or a rebuild pops. WebGPU allows eight vertex buffers per material, which the full crown is close to.
- Sites live on `SITE_CELL` cells, deterministic per cell, thinned by `SITE_ODDS`, the biome's welcome and the fit of the ground; `sunward` types face the sunrise azimuth from `SKY_EVENTS`. Site records, and prop records for kinds that declare `obstacle`, are obstacles for the bike and the camera like trees.
- The ground shade sheet is painted in world order and must upload with `flipY` off. The shadow frame follows the bike in whole shadow-map texels across the light's plane; do not trim shadow casters by distance from the bike (the frame is a box across the light but an open tube along it, and dawn would break).

## Ride, steering and camera

- The bike rides itself on the right-hand lane of `world.road`, looking `RIDE.look` seconds ahead and slowing for bends by `RIDE.grip`. Right-drag or touch takes the bars (`takeBars`): the heading is the viewer's, the bike may leave the road, and after `RIDE.hold` seconds of let-go it turns back to the road and rejoins it on the way it faced. Heading stays wrapped. `state.dir` is the direction along the road and never flips on its own.
- The bike stands on the drawn ground: pitch from the two tyre heights, lean from the turn rate, wheels from the odometer. `src/bike.js` returns a tree of hinged nodes (`nodes`), which the engine turns into groups under `bike.userData.rig`; `animateBike` drives them (suspension, bars, rider, head, arms by two-bone reach). Keep the bike and rider under `BIKE.triangles`; the measured count is `__ride.bikeTriangles`.
- The camera has no director and no spring: it is placed rigidly on the bike every frame, so the bike is the orbit pivot. The viewer's orbit persists; never add auto-recentering. Left, right and wheel follow World of Warcraft. The vertical field of view widens on tall screens (`fitFov`) so a phone keeps the horizontal view a landscape screen has.
- The opening (`INTRO`, `updateIntro`) and the title card (`TITLE`, `updateTitle`) play for every world that is not resumed, driven from `state.t` in `advance`, never by CSS transitions, so a pause holds them and the checks can step them. A fresh world's start is chosen from candidates along the road (`START`), on land and gentle grades, in the coast or the paddies when either is in reach.
- Tests read `__ride` (state, cam, road, world, step, biomeAt, biomeWeights, paint, bikeTriangles, capture).

## Page, memory and rendering

- The page opens on a white veil (`#loading`) and lifts it only from `unveil`, after the first frame's GPU work is done (the queue on WebGPU, a fence on WebGL2) and a browser frame has passed; `ready` and the enabled Begin flip together there, and `openWorld` in the checks waits for `ready`, so nothing may show the world or enable Begin earlier. The gate (`#begin`) carries only the button and a hint, with no render loop behind it; the failure hook in the page reuses the veil.
- Memory lives in two localStorage keys (`the-long-ride-settings` and `the-long-ride-resume`), settings and resume, read once at load through `remember` and validated field by field; a URL seed always beats the remembered world. Flight saves are throttled by wall clock and flushed on pause, hide, leave and dispose, never before Begin. The browser checks therefore clear storage and run inside a fresh frame; a check that reloads the top page would lose its own script.
- Work is bounded: whole-tree instancing per species, reduced distant crowns, a local grass window, small pools for boulders and sites, and fixed-size terrain storage, so world geometry never accumulates. Rendering is capped at two million pixels and 1.5 device pixels per CSS pixel. Only the scene pass is multisampled, the sky dome draws last among the opaque objects, and everything past tone mapping stays in eight bits; `docs/perf-notes.md` says why. Shader time follows the simulation clock. Even one-shot redraws go through `setAnimationLoop`; direct calls reuse cached TSL FRAME passes. Pause and visibility handlers stop rendering and suspend audio; audio is Begin-gated; teardown must finish pending GPU readbacks before destroying their buffers, then release graphics resources and close audio.
- Cost is measured, not guessed: `tools/bench.js` and `tools/parity.js` (commands in `CONTRIBUTING.md`) show what a change costs and whether the picture moved, and `docs/perf-notes.md` records what they found. Read it before optimizing anything and add to it after. One trap from it belongs here: do not trim the shadow map's casters by distance from the bike. The frame is a 720 m box across the light but an open tube along it, from the shadow camera's near plane a kilometre up-light to its far plane beyond, so a tree well outside any sphere can still be the caster a low sun throws across the frame, and dawn is the moment that would break.
- `tools/capture-launch-media.mjs` records the launch film and the hero still from the page itself, headless, and ships nothing; `CONTRIBUTING.md` says how to run it and `media/` is ignored. Two things in it are load-bearing. The page's own veil, gate and controls must stay painted while the engine's canvas is being copied: with nothing over it the canvas is composited straight to the screen and each copy costs a whole frame, so the recording leaves those layers out of its composite instead of hiding them. And the recorded sound must be the page's own destination tap: bridging it into a second `AudioContext` runs on a second clock, under-runs, and loses a third of the samples. `assets/hero.png` is updated by hand from what the tool writes.

## Approved looks

Each was chosen by eye and is the reference a change is compared against: a change to any of them shows its before and after at the same seed and vantage.

- **The soft illustrated rendering** (`src/color-grade.js`): broad diffuse lighting bands without a physical specular lobe, restrained bump, softened shadows, ACES, edge-preserving reconstruction and FXAA; dawn, dusk and night keep their own palettes.
- **The jade water** (`src/water.js`, record `tests/water-review.md`) and **the star-led Milky Way** (`src/milky-way.js`, `src/galaxy-matter.js`, record `tests/milky-way-review.md`): inherited from the original engine, unchanged. No browser check guards the sky, water or night looks; they are verified by eye, plus `node tests/galaxy-checks.mjs` for the Milky Way's generated light.
- **The sky through a day**: more palette keys around dawn and dusk than at noon, a sun-side band, a moon with its own lit face, painted clouds.
- **Original procedural art throughout**: painted canvas textures, curved branches, needle, frond and petal cards, grass; every tree one baked trunk, limbs and crown of painted cards; the bike lofted and swept, never boxes. Nothing is downloaded.

## Development and testing

Running the page in a real browser can be resource intensive. Any browser tab created for development and testing must be torn down as soon as it is no longer needed. Run `tests/ride-checks.js` through a real browser after engine changes, on both backends, three seeds and the bundle (commands in `CONTRIBUTING.md`); serve with `Cache-Control: no-store` or module imports will be cached across reloads.

Keep this file for knowledge useful to almost every future agent session in this project. Do not repeat what the codebase already shows; point to the authoritative file or command instead. Prefer rewriting or pruning existing entries over appending new ones, and keep entries concise.
